Where we actually are

Sortey is further along than the planning docs suggest. Destination trips (lodging, rooms, member transit, ground transport) are complete on web and mobile. The road-trip planner has trip days, replan drafts, fuel and overnight zones, route gradient, dual-candidate routes, and predicted stops. Offline-first mobile has outboxes for journey stops, fuel logs, expenses, and pins, plus a trip offline pack. Corridor Cast shipped end-to-end and then gained provenance-backed grounding and a second model provider.

What is not done splits cleanly in two: a launch that never quite finished, and the differentiator that was specced but never built. Everything below is ordered around that split.

Goals & non-goals

Goals

  • Finish the launch. A build sitting in App Store Connect that nobody outside the household can install is not shipped.
  • Make the van's real state drive the plan. This is the north star from the DayMap spec and the one thing notes-plus-maps genuinely cannot do.
  • Make Corridor Cast dependable rather than impressive-once: native playback, real offline, and a quality floor that survives prompt edits.
  • Keep every operational claim grounded. Tier-1 facts from trip data, tier-1.5 from sourced research, everything else hedged.

Non-goals

  • Booking flights, hotels, or campsites. Sortey plans; it does not transact.
  • Turn-by-turn navigation. Replan is not reroute; Maps stays the navigator.
  • Multi-currency settlement, real-time collaborative cursors, a full SQLite mirror. All explicitly deferred and still deferred.
  • Redistributing iOverlander POI data. Workspace-scoped only, permanently.

Two real blockers

Device reinstall window. The native player needs expo-audio in the standalone trip-device build, and a mid-trip rebuild-and-reinstall is impractical. This waits for a trip to end.

A funded model provider. As of today neither provider can generate: no Anthropic key on the worker, and the Gemini account is on prepaid billing with a depleted balance. The pipeline is verified end-to-end except the model call.

What is already proven, so it does not need re-litigating

Read gate, claim-with-lease, per-segment R2 checkpoints, frame-level MP3 concat, the scan-only duration walker, the authenticated audio route with its enumeration guard, checkpoint cleanup on success, and fail-fast on provider config or billing errors — all verified against production. A 3-chapter episode synthesized in about 21 seconds: 3,383 characters billed, 3.2 MB, 204.77 s, and the walker's duration matched afinfo exactly.

Exit criteria. An episode generated the night before plays in the van with the phone locked, in airplane mode, with chapter titles — and its documentary claims trace to sources a passenger can read.

M3 — The predictive day

Planned v0.5.0.0

The north star: the van's real state drives the plan. DriftPort answers "can the van support what we are about to do?" — Sortey turns that into where to stop, when to service, and what today looks like. This is the milestone that makes Sortey better than notes plus maps.

Correction — 2026-08-02

This milestone was written from the DayMap spec rather than from the code, and it over-scoped as a result. daymap/consumption.ts (learned rates), daymap/service.ts (predictServiceNeeds, matchServiceStops), and daymap/briefing.ts already exist. The genuine gap was narrower: matching was nearest-by-straight-line from the current position, with no notion of route position — so it could recommend a stop already driven past — and there was no clustering. daymap/service-run.ts now covers both. VanState persistence (van_state_reading) also already exists, keyed by trip.

Reading the spec is not reading the system. Same mistake the stale claims in STATUS.md make.

Cross-cutting tracks

These do not belong to a milestone; they run continuously and each one already has evidence behind it from this month's work.

Cost governance

Corridor Cast spends real money per episode — model tokens plus voice characters. Token counters are persisted even on failed runs and checkpoints prevent double-billing, but there is still no ceiling. A per-trip monthly cap enforced before the call belongs in M2, and a spend view belongs wherever the operator looks first.

Reliability and observability

The cron pump is the only always-on machinery and its failures are currently visible only in the job row. Worker logs carry pre-existing OpenTelemetry export timeouts that make real errors harder to spot. Worth one focused pass: fix the OTel export, then alert on pump errors rather than discovering them by polling.

Delivery platform

Deploys now run through forge deploy create production --target worker, pinned to a node that actually has the toolchain. Two upstream ForgeGraph bugs found this month are fixed on a local branch but deliberately unpushed: stack-sync hardcoding main as the PR base, and a target delete that returned an opaque 500 when deployment history referenced it. Landing those removes the manual retarget dance from every PR.

Data licensing

iOverlander POIs cannot be redistributed. Every corridor query filters by workspace and must keep doing so — this constraint touches the cast context pack, poi-suggest, briefing, and the corridor router, and it does not relax at any milestone.

Verification strategy

  • Unit and router tests stay the floor — 589 in @sortey/api today, and every new domain rule arrives with one.
  • Evals for generated content. Prompt changes cannot be regression-tested by reading; M2's eval harness is what makes cast prompts safe to iterate.
  • Real-device passes for anything touching GPS, motion mode, or offline. These have repeatedly revealed what emulators do not.
  • Production probes over assumptions: query the job row, pull the object from R2, check the byte count and duration. Every claim in the M2 "already proven" note was established that way.

Open questions

  1. Does Corridor Cast ship to other people, or stay a household feature? If it ships, cost governance and voice licensing move from M2 into M1.
  2. Is DriftPort a hard dependency or an enrichment? The roadmap assumes enrichment. If it is a dependency, M3 needs the hardware schedule attached to it.
  3. What is the second trip? M3 is unverifiable without one, and its shape determines whether service logistics or the work-window finder matters more.
  4. Do the ForgeGraph fixes land? They are written and tested but unpushed. Until they do, every PR needs a manual base retarget.
  5. Is v1.0 gated on multi-tenancy at all? An alternative reading: v1.0 is "one household, flawlessly", and group scale is v2.
